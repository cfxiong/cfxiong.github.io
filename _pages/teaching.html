Teaching portfolio

Teaching transportation as a living system.

I modernize transportation engineering education by connecting field observation, simulation, experiments, research seminars, and mentored student projects. Students learn transportation not only as formulas and design standards, but as a system they can measure, model, question, and improve.

01 / Modernizing the field

From lectures to lived transportation systems.

My courses use field studies, simulation modeling, experiments, and project-based learning to make transportation engineering tangible. Students collect observations, test design ideas, and connect classroom methods to real mobility systems around campus and the Philadelphia region.

  • Field studies that put students in direct contact with traffic operations, transit service, and roadway design.
  • Hands-on modules using simulation, data analysis, and system design exercises.
  • Student presentations that turn technical observation into evidence-based engineering judgment.

02 / Research in the classroom

Bringing students close to high-level transportation research.

I invite scholars and practitioners into the classroom through seminars, guest lectures, and workshops so that undergraduate and graduate students can see how research questions are formed, debated, and translated into practice.

  • Recent visitors and examples include Kara Kockelman, Jaeyoung Lee, Chaoru Lu, and Paul Comfort.
  • Topics span pedestrian and bicycle safety, mobility analytics, transit practice, modeling, infrastructure, and technology adoption.
  • The goal is to make research culture visible and approachable early in students' training.

03 / Research pathways

Designing research projects for students at different entry points.

I build scaffolded research experiences for high school, undergraduate, and graduate students, including the Villanova Summer Research Internship, Match Program, Sophomore Research Scholar, and Villanova Summer Research Scholarship. These projects give students a real path from curiosity to research output.

6 undergraduate students have contributed to paper publications through mentored transportation research projects.
